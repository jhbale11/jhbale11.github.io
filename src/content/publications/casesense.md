Legal case research requires combining facts, legal principles, and timelines into persuasive arguments. Yet existing retrieval tools return one-dimensional ranked lists, collapsing this structure into a single relevance score. A formative study with five lawyers confirmed this flat output leads to missed cases, temporal gaps, and fragmented reasoning. We present CaseSense, a visual analytics system for multi-dimensional exploration via tri-axial mapping with dynamic weighting, anchor-based navigation, and purpose-constrained view projections. A within-subjects study with 16 legal professionals compared CaseSense against conventional workflows. CaseSense achieved complete key-case discovery in the Fact Synthesis task, with the largest gains in organization and connection identification. Participants adopted task-matched interaction strategies without instructions. These findings suggest encoding domain reasoning dimensions as manipulable spatial axes supports expert sensemaking, while separating spatial organization from AI-generated content preserves verification behavior.
