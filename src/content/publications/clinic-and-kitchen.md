Dialysis patients receive dietary constraints in the clinic, based on their lab results, but have to act on them at home, in the kitchen. This poster frames a constraint-aware retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) chatbot as *boundary infrastructure* that sits between those two worlds: it grounds its answers in each patient's clinical lab values and the care team's dietary rules, and translates them into everyday food decisions in the patient's own context.

The study moved through four phases, from formative interviews with patients and clinicians, through design and deployment of the chatbot, to debriefs with stakeholders on both sides of the boundary. It complements the randomized controlled trial reported in [our JMIR paper](/publications/ckd-rag-chatbot-rct) with a CSCW perspective on how such a system mediates between clinical and domestic work.
