ToneCanvas treats the tone of a character's voice as something a writer can *paint* onto a manuscript rather than describe in a prompt. Inside the manuscript editor, watercolor-style overlays show where each character's tone is applied. A tone palette with sliders lets the writer tune attributes and see a live preview of the rewritten passage, and character layers hold reusable tone chips so that the same voice can be applied consistently across chapters.

The system targets narrative consistency, one of the persistent failure modes of LLM-assisted fiction writing, by giving authors a direct, visual handle on character tone and terminology. The poster was accepted to the UIST 2026 adjunct track.
